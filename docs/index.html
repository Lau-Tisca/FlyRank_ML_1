Applied Research Paper Lane 2: Content Refresh & Opportunity

Which Pages Should Be Reviewed First? Prioritizing Search Performance Decay on 79M Daily Records

Track: Applied Machine Learning Capstone Dataset Release: FlyRank v20260703 Status: Validated Decision-Support Artifact

Abstract

Managing large enterprise content inventories requires identifying decaying or underperforming pages without wasting limited editorial bandwidth. In this study, we formulate content opportunity discovery as a client-grouped ranking task using a longitudinal warehouse of 78.8 million daily search and engagement records across 104 pseudonymized enterprise domains. We evaluate deterministic heuristic baselines against decision trees, regularized logistic regression, and random forest classifiers evaluated strictly under client-holdout cross-validation to prevent cross-domain data leakage. Our measured results demonstrate that an ensemble Random Forest model achieves a Precision@50 of 0.740 and ROC AUC of 0.780, representing an observed 1.61× precision lift over hand-crafted rule baselines (0.460) and a 3.13× lift over the empirical holdout base rate (0.236). Finally, we translate model probability outputs into an operational content action playbook with granular reason codes, strict human-review safety guardrails, and non-production decision-support boundaries.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Enterprise digital publication libraries frequently span thousands of indexable URLs. Over time, content undergoes organic traffic decay due to query intent shifts, algorithmic re-ranking, and competitor publication sprints. However, editorial bandwidth is strictly constrained: typical enterprise teams can only manually review and refresh 20 to 50 URLs per month.

Traditional SEO heuristics rely on arbitrary global thresholds (e.g., "flag pages older than 180 days with declining traffic"). In multi-client environments, these naive rules suffer from extreme failure modes:

  • Scale Disparity: Large-inventory clients flood the global review queue, while small-inventory sites are entirely crowded out.
  • High False Positive Rates: Static rules trigger costly human rewrites ($150–$300 per page) on healthy pages experiencing routine search volume seasonality.

The Core Question: Can we reliably rank content opportunity candidates to maximize editorial yield (Precision@K) while avoiding target leakage across cross-client domains?

Furthermore, non-GA4 tracked pages (ga4_data_available = FALSE) and low-exposure noise pages (impressions < 50) were excluded to maintain signal validity.

3. Methodology & Validation Design

3.1 Label Definition & Temporal Windows

To prevent future-window leakage, we structure our feature extraction and label evaluation on distinct, non-overlapping temporal windows:

  • Feature Window ($T_{\text{feat}}$): Trailing 15 days (e.g., Days 1–15 of the evaluation partition).
  • Outcome/Label Window ($T_{\text{target}}$): Subsequent 16 days (e.g., Days 16–31).

The target proxy label $\text{Is\_Opportunity}$ is binary, identifying pages experiencing substantial traffic decay ($>20\%$ decline in organic clicks) despite holding proven search exposure:

$$\text{Target} = \mathbf{1}\left(\sum_{t \in T_{\text{target}}} \text{Clicks}_t < 0.80 \times \sum_{t \in T_{\text{feat}}} \text{Clicks}_t \land \sum_{t \in T_{\text{feat}}} \text{Impressions}_t \ge 50\right)$$

3.2 Feature Vector

Each feature is guaranteed knowable at the decision moment:

  1. log_impressions: $\log(1 + \text{Impressions}_{15d})$ (Historical demand envelope).
  2. log_clicks: $\log(1 + \text{Clicks}_{15d})$ (Pre-intervention traffic baseline).
  3. avg_position: Mean SERP position across the feature window.
  4. ctr_feature: Measured click-through rate ($\text{Clicks} / \text{Impressions} \times 100$).
  5. tracking_depth_days: Number of distinct active tracking dates in the period.
  6. ga4_sessions: Log-transformed verified on-site user engagement.

3.3 Client-Grouped Validation Design

Standard random k-fold splitting produces severe metric over-optimism because content items from the same domain share domain authority and indexing history. We enforce strict Client-Grouped Holdout Validation: all content items from test clients are completely quarantined during training.

4. Measured Results & Model Comparison

Models were trained and evaluated on identical client-holdout test splits (9,275 rows across 6 holdout domains). Precision at Top-$K$ ($K=50, 100$) serves as the primary operational metric, reflecting the actual weekly capacity of human editorial teams.

Model Architecture ROC AUC Avg Precision Precision@50 Precision@100 Recall F1 Score
Hand-Crafted Rule Baseline 0.650 0.304 0.460 0.390 0.388 0.349

Decision Tree Classifier 0.765 0.378 0.500 0.500 0.094 0.159
Random Forest (Champion) 0.780 0.417 0.740 0.660 0.050 0.093
Key Finding: The Random Forest model achieved a Precision@50 of 0.740 and ROC AUC of 0.780 on the unseen client-holdout split, delivering an observed 1.61× precision lift over the heuristic rule baseline (0.460) and a 3.13× lift over the holdout dataset base rate (0.236). Non-linear models successfully disentangle position-CTR interaction effects that static linear rules misclassify.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • Observational Decision Support: High model scores indicate statistical correlation with historical traffic decay; they do not constitute a causal guarantee that an editorial rewrite will reclaim search rank.
  • Unbalanced Client Histories: Tracking depth differs across clients depending on when GA4 integration was activated.
  • Exogenous Search Volatility: The model cannot anticipate unannounced Google Search Core Algorithm updates or competitor backlink acquisition campaigns.

6. Operational Action Playbook

Model probabilities are translated into five concrete editorial archetypes:

Archetype Operational Reason Code Recommended Workflow Action Review Tier
Stale High-Exposure Winner RC_STALE_HIGH_IMP REWRITE_EXPAND: Comprehensive content refresh and fact-checking. Standard Editorial
SERP CTR Mismatch RC_SERP_CTR_GAP SERP_TITLE_META_FIX: Optimize snippet, title, and metadata. Standard Editorial
Thin Content Underperformer RC_THIN_HIGH_EXPOSURE EXPAND_DEPTH: Add technical depth, diagrams, and citations. Standard Editorial
High-Traffic Top 3 Page TOP_3_POSITION_GUARD MANUAL_AUDIT_ONLY: Restrict automated intervention. Mandatory Senior Lead
Healthy Baseline RC_HEALTHY MONITOR_STABLE: Maintain state; no editorial intervention. None
⛔ The Strict NO-GO Guardrails (Never Automate):
  1. No Automated AI Publishing: Do not auto-generate and deploy text without human domain review.
  2. No Automated Pruning or 301 Redirects: Modifying canonical status or URL structures requires SEO lead authorization.
  3. YMYL Domain Exclusions: Medical, legal, and financial guidance pages must receive professional subject-matter sign-off.
